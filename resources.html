FROM THE RESOURCE SHELF

The Analytical Writing Toolkit

  • Point: Make a clear claim about a specific choice the writer makes.
  • Evidence: Use a short, relevant quotation to support your point.
  • Effect: Explain how the choice shapes meaning, tone, or the reader’s response in this particular context.
  • Link: Connect your explanation to purpose, audience, or your comparison.
  • For comparative writing: Connect both texts within your paragraph. Explain a meaningful similarity or difference, rather than listing features.
FROM THE RESOURCE SHELF

The Purposeful Writer’s Checklist

  • Audience: Who will read this? What do they know, need, or care about?
  • Purpose: What should the reader think, feel, understand, or do?
  • Form: Which conventions suit this text type?
  • Structure: Does each paragraph move the writing forward?
  • Language: Do vocabulary, tone, and sentence choices suit the task?
  • Review: Check clarity, accuracy, and the task’s word guidance.
FROM THE RESOURCE SHELF

Words That Connect Ideas

  • Compare: Similarly · Likewise · In a comparable way
  • Contrast: In contrast · Conversely · Whereas · While
  • Develop: Furthermore · In addition · More specifically
  • Explain an effect: This suggests… · This emphasizes… · This positions the reader to…
  • Qualify: To some extent · Although · Arguably
  • Use transitions for a clear relationship between ideas. Precise explanation matters more than an impressive word.
